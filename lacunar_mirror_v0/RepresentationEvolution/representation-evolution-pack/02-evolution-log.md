# Evolution Log

This file records the major representational changes in approximate chronological order.

---

## 01 — Physics: plumbing or hypothesis?

**Initial representation**

The physics layer was treated as instrumentation plumbing.

**Resistance**

Choosing which timing distinctions to preserve is already a theoretical commitment.

**Refactoring**

The physics layer is instrumentation, but it is also a hypothesis about which distinctions in raw telemetry are worth preserving.

**Result**

Instrumentation became accountable rather than neutral.

---

## 02 — Diagnostics split into observables and structures

**Initial representation**

Diagnostics sat between physics and visualisation.

**Resistance**

The word combined measurable quantities with relationships and interpretations.

**Refactoring**

- Observables: measurable quantities.
- Structures: relationships among observables.
- Diagnostics: evaluations based on those structures.
- Interpretation: proposed meaning.

**Result**

Measurement error became more localisable.

---

## 03 — Sessions separated from transitions

**Initial representation**

Sessions and transitions were treated as similar structural objects.

**Resistance**

A session partitions time. A transition is an event.

**Refactoring**

Sessions and transitions became distinct concepts.

**Result**

Partitioning logic and dynamic-event logic could fail independently.

---

## 04 — Methodology separated from constraints

**Initial representation**

Methodology was treated as the stable epistemic foundation.

**Resistance**

Methodology must be allowed to evolve.

**Refactoring**

The invariant is not the methodology itself, but the constraints under which methodology may evolve.

**Result**

Revision became legitimate without making all change equally legitimate.

---

## 05 — Feedback separated from anomaly

**Initial representation**

Observations were assumed to flow upward through the architecture as feedback.

**Resistance**

Routine observations should not continuously rewrite higher layers.

**Refactoring**

Only anomalies or meaningful resistance should propagate upward.

**Result**

The architecture stopped treating ordinary operation as a reason for conceptual redesign.

---

## 06 — Simplicity replaced by fidelity

**Initial representation**

A successful refactoring was thought to simplify the architecture.

**Resistance**

Reality is not required to be elegant.

**Refactoring**

The real criterion became fidelity. Simplicity is only a proxy.

**Result**

Complexity could be retained when the phenomenon genuinely required it.

---

## 07 — Concepts became compressions

**Initial representation**

Concepts were treated mainly as explanatory tools.

**Resistance**

Every concept discards structure in order to become usable.

**Refactoring**

A concept is a provisional compression.

**Key question**

> What have we compressed that reality keeps trying to pull apart?

**Result**

Definitions became auditable for what they omit.

---

## 08 — Error became surprise

**Initial representation**

Systems should provide legitimate places for error to go.

**Resistance**

Before something is classified as an error, it first appears as surprise.

**Refactoring**

Design systems so that surprise has somewhere legitimate to go.

**Result**

Appeal, defer, audit, anomaly handling, and falsification became related channels rather than afterthoughts.

---

## 09 — Reveals became encounters

**Initial representation**

Representations reveal their distortions.

**Resistance**

That wording assigned agency in the wrong direction.

**Refactoring**

Representations encounter resistance. Reality exposes the representation.

**Result**

Accountability remained directed from the world toward the model.

---

## 10 — Continuous refactoring became event-driven

**Initial representation**

Good reasoning appeared to require constant monitoring for conceptual overload.

**Resistance**

This creates an unsustainable cognitive tax.

**Refactoring**

Refactoring is event-driven. It becomes worthwhile when patches, caveats, or friction cost more than redesign.

**Result**

The method became compatible with bounded rationality.

---

## 11 — Patch saturation separated from prediction error

**Initial representation**

Refactoring might trigger from either structural mess or empirical failure.

**Resistance**

These are not competing alternatives.

**Refactoring**

- Patch saturation: internal structural debt.
- Prediction error: external mismatch.
- Deadline pressure: operational constraint.

**Result**

Structure, evidence, and execution became separate dimensions.

---

## 12 — Sequential gates became multidimensional admissibility

**Initial representation**

Structure, evidence, and execution were represented as sequential checks.

**Resistance**

A sequential model could allow one dimension to be evaluated in isolation.

**Refactoring**

A proposed transition may be assessed across structural, empirical, and operational admissibility.

**Status**

Promising conceptual model, not validated specification.

---

## 13 — Runtime gate became temporal binding

**Initial representation**

Admissibility was assumed to occur at runtime.

**Resistance**

Analog control, tacit action, and creative generation appeared to fall outside the model.

**Refactoring**

Admissibility may be paid or bound at:

- design time;
- learning time;
- deployment;
- execution;
- audit or retrospective review.

**Result**

The apparent counterexamples exposed runtime bias rather than necessarily disproving the pattern.

---

## 14 — Where cost is paid became who pays

**Initial representation**

The key question was: where was the cost of admissibility paid?

**Resistance**

Temporal location does not identify burden or liability.

**Refactoring**

Ask:

- When is the cost paid?
- Who pays it?
- Where in the architecture?
- In what currency?
- Could the payer meaningfully intervene?

**Result**

The inquiry touched political economy and risk externalisation.

---

## 15 — Conservation law weakened

**Initial representation**

Cost of admissibility is constant.

**Resistance**

Good architecture can genuinely reduce total cost.

**Refactoring**

Unaddressed admissibility cost does not disappear merely because it is not visible at execution. It may reappear as risk, friction, exclusion, or liability elsewhere.

**Result**

The metaphor was retained without pretending it was a physical law.

---

## 16 — Alignment theorem weakened to heuristic

**Initial representation**

System integrity was expressed as equivalent to alignment among benefit, authority, visibility, and liability.

**Resistance**

Alignment is neither sufficient nor proven necessary for all forms of integrity.

**Refactoring**

Misalignment among benefit, authority, visibility, and liability is a strong indicator of governance risk.

**Result**

A biconditional theorem became a testable heuristic.

---

## 17 — Human restraint became distributed epistemic functions

**Initial representation**

AI expands; the human supplies restraint and stopping.

**Resistance**

The interaction showed that generation, criticism, curation, and stopping were not fixed to identities.

**Refactoring**

Possible epistemic functions include:

- expansion;
- resistance;
- refinement;
- promotion control;
- curation;
- stopping;
- return to reality.

These functions may be distributed across humans and models.

**Result**

The stopping condition was relocated from one participant to the interaction.

---

## 18 — Product became process

**Initial representation**

The main output was a better framework.

**Resistance**

The most interesting evidence concerned how the framework changed.

**Refactoring**

The object of study became representation evolution under distributed resistance.

**Current status**

An interesting observation requiring further cases.
