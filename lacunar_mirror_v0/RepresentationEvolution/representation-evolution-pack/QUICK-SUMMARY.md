# Quick Summary

## Where this came from

The inquiry began during preparation for **Lacunar Mirror diagnostic system v0.4**.

The practical concern was roughly:

> How should the diagnostic system be structured without allowing interpretation to contaminate observation?

While working through that question, several overloaded concepts were separated. The conversation then became an inquiry into the process that produced those separations.

## What happened

A recurring operation appeared:

1. Begin with a representation.
2. Encounter resistance.
3. Look for hidden compression or misplaced responsibility.
4. Refactor the representation.
5. Check whether the change reduces meaningful distortion.
6. Refuse to promote the result beyond the available evidence.
7. Stop when another abstraction would no longer improve fidelity.
8. Return the question to evidence, implementation, or another source of resistance.

## Central working question

> **What representation would allow reality to disagree with us more precisely?**

## Important observations

- Concepts behave like compressions rather than permanent truths.
- Repeated caveats can indicate conceptual overload.
- Separating responsibilities can improve error locality.
- Internal coherence is necessary but insufficient.
- Independent reasoners can act as sources of resistance.
- Language models may drift toward premature closure and promotion.
- Stopping is not the absence of more ideas; it is the absence of more earned ideas.
- The stopping condition may emerge from the interaction rather than belonging to one participant.
- The process may distribute epistemic functions such as expansion, criticism, refinement, curation, and stopping.

## Current status

Interesting and documented.

Not validated.

The next meaningful step is to observe whether the process helps in other real design problems.
